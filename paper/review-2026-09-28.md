## 2. Conjecture 1.4

### 2.1 Divisibility

- Route C is the right proof to publish. It uses Poisson summation over C = C^⊥, the transforms
  φ̂ = i√3·φ and ι̂ = 3δ₀ − 1, and "weights of self-orthogonal codes are ≡ 0 mod 3". The sign of
  (i√3)^{n−2} is irrelevant, as the remark "what the proof uses" in `divisibility.tex` §4 says.
- The hypothesis n ≥ 1 (found by the formalization) is stated in all three places it should be
  (`divisibility.tex`, notes §0, Lean).
- Nothing to simplify further. The other routes (neighbours; modular forms; GJF's Borcherds argument)
  belong in remarks: route B is worth one paragraph because it gives the lattice statement (odd
  unimodular L of rank 12k with shadow minimum ≥ k), and route A because its neighbour identity
  (★) is used again for Theorem 5.4 and Proposition 5.5.

### 2.2 Existence

- The proof in `index24.tex` is correct and close to minimal: the engine must add 24 per step
  (|σ″| = 2|𝓑| is even), so two base cases (b = 1 for even k, b = 2 for odd k) are needed, and the
  base is a 729-word computation.
- A conceptual reformulation that may help readers (optional, a remark): per block, Q_σ substitutes a
  fixed 16-coordinate *gadget*. Γ := h₂h₁(0 ⊕ T ⊕ T²) ⊂ F₃^16 is self-orthogonal of dimension 6, the map
  u ↦ h₂h₁(u, 0, 0) + Γ identifies F₃⁴ isometrically with Γ^⊥/Γ (two anti-isometries compose to an
  isometry; one alone would not, which is why a single doubling fails). Lemma A (summing over the T²
  part) followed by Lemma B (summing over the T part) says that the coset h₂h₁(u,0,0) + Γ has signed
  full-weight count λ(wt u). So Q_σ(E) is the gluing of E with b copies of Γ, its index is
  Σ_e ∏_{singletons} φ(e_i) ∏_β λ(wt e|_β) by the usual gluing formula, and "+12 per block" is 16 − 4.
  Using Lemma B at the second step as well shows that the same coset counts come out when one or both
  halves of the gadget are re-read as two λ-blocks, which is what lets the construction iterate.

### 2.3 An open item closed: R_σ(E) ≡ Index(E) (mod 48)

CLAUDE.md "Next steps" 3 asks for this; the Theorem 5.4 argument does carry over [P; V:
`review_rsigma_mod48.py`].
- Put Λ = φ^{⊗s} ⊗ λ^{⊗b}. Then Λ̂ = (i√3)^s 9^b Λ = i^s 3^{m/2} Λ, and s = m − 4b ≡ m ≡ 0 (mod 4), so
  Λ̂ = 3^{m/2}Λ, the eigenvalue of f.
- Λ is supported on isotropic vectors: a word with Λ ≠ 0 has weight s + Σ_β w_β with w_β ∈ {1, 4}, which
  is ≡ s + b = m − 3b ≡ 0 (mod 3) for 3 | m. And Λ(−x) = Λ(x) since s is even.
- So the neighbour identity holds for Σ Λ. The rest is the proof of Theorem 5.4 verbatim: Λ − f is even
  on every word, ±x pair off, the base code (tetracodes) has Λ = f = 0, and both R_σ(E) = Index(Q_σE)
  and Index(E) lie in 24ℤ.
- This explains why R_σ(Golay) only takes the values ±24 and ±72 (all ≡ 24 mod 48), and it means a seed
  pair (E, σ) can only have |R_σ| = 24 if Index(E) ≡ 24 (mod 48): a free necessary condition for seed
  searches. E₇₂ (Index −1608 ≡ 24 mod 48, R = 24) is consistent.

## 3. The Main Question

### 3.1 Code level

- The equivariant construction is right, including the two subtleties the blueprint found and fixed:
  invariant tetracode placements must exist at *both* steps (a stabiliser acting on a split pair by an
  involutive swap or by diag(1, −1) forbids them, [EQ] Prop 4.10), and the refinement and σ″-splits
  must be the *transposed* ones, or the chains skip residue classes ([EQ] Lemma 4.11).
- Lemma 4.3 (fixed codes collapse; T_h(Q_σE) = R_σ̄(D)) re-derived: the fixed space of a transported
  triple of tetracodes is one tetracode after collapsing, the Hadamard maps commute with collapsing, and
  f is preserved because φ³ = φ.
- `MQcode k` in `lean/GJF/TierA.lean` matches GJF Thm 5.6's hypotheses: for m ∈ {3, 6, 12} it asks for
  g^m = 1, g^{m/2} = −1 (m even), g^{m/3} without fixed coordinate (hence of shape 3^{n/3}) and
  T_{g^{m/3}} = 0 with the *signed* action; the order of g is then exactly m. For m = 8 and 24 it asks
  for the Q₈ and Q₈ × ℤ/3 relations, including KJ = −JK (needed: J² = K² = −1 alone does not give Q₈).
- Rebuilt from scratch in a fresh container: `lake build GJF.TierA` succeeds (34 min on 4 cores, of
  which the three ℤ/12 certificate files take 17–32 min each). `#print axioms GJF.Ex.tierA` gives only
  `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`; the one numerical input enters as the explicit hypothesis
  `E72Hyp : R E0 = 24`. I re-ran that sum with `rsigma_is` (information-set enumeration, 2³⁶ patterns):
  +24.

### 3.2 Orbifold index (Theorem O)

- Correct given ORB. The best idea in it is Lemma 3.3: for g of even order m with g^{m/2} = −1 and a
  fixed-point-free coordinate permutation, the eigenvalues of g are the odd powers of e^{2πi/m}, each
  2n/m times, so the g-twisted Ramond sector has lowest weight n/24 + n/(12m²) > c/24 and contributes
  nothing. This needs no modular transformation. (For the ℤ/12 generator on n = 36 the margin is
  36/1728 = 0.021: small but exact.)
- Only the order-3 sectors use modular covariance, as in GJF.

### 3.3 Anomalies

- Cyclic groups: GJF Ex 4.4 as used in GJF Thm 5.6. The blueprint's list of GJF typos (G1: for m odd
  the exponent is 2m/k; G2: each block contributes 6 × #sign changes) and the citation gap (G3: the m = 3
  step cites a thesis on even lattices; closed by [AN] Lemma 2.7, Prop 2.8) is accurate.
- **Q₈ (new).** The shortest correct proof is the one assembled in [AN] §4 (not the one used as the main
  proof in `main-question.tex`):
  1. maj(α) = 0 (N₋(J) = N₋(K) = 6k even); α|⟨x⟩ = 2k ∈ ℤ/8 for x ∈ {J, K, JK} and α|⟨−1⟩ = 2k ∈ ℤ/4
     (GJF Ex 4.4). So 4 ∤ k ⇒ anomalous, and for 4 | k, α ∈ K := ker(rSH³(Q₈) → ⊕_x rSH³(⟨x⟩)).
  2. |K| = 2: |rSH³(Q₈)| = 32 by the layer sequence, and the restriction image has order 16 (generated
     by the images of the H⁴ generator and two free-fermion classes). Checked by hand.
  3. k ≡ 4 (mod 8) ⇒ α ≠ 0: if the orbifold W existed, its NS character would be S-invariant, but
     ch W|S − ch W = ¼(3N − T_ψ)(F − F|S) with N = 2^{3k}e^{2πik/8} and T_ψ = 2^{3k}Σ_x ψ(x); this vanishes
     only for 8 | k, ψ = 1. The code C enters only through Z(1,1), which is S-invariant. Re-derived by
     hand and checked numerically with fresh code (`review_q8_character.py`).
  4. 8 | k ⇒ α = 0: with D the Q₈-invariant tetracode sum of length 48, α_C = α_{C⊕D} − α_D = κ − κ = 0.
  This needs neither the coprime-gauging lemma nor eta invariants. The free-fermion reduction
  (α = 3k·a, a of order 8, 5/8 on S³/Q₈) is then a consistency check and an explanation, not an input.
- Q₈ × ℤ/3: Sylow detection ([AN] Prop 3.2) uses only naturality and injectivity of the layer maps and
  transfer for ordinary cohomology. Correct.

## 5. Simplifications

**S1. The eta identity behind μ, without Dedekind sums** [P; V: `review_q8_character.py`]. With
f = (η(2τ)/η(4τ))^{n/2}, g = f|S and F = (η(τ)η(4τ)/η(2τ)²)^{n/2}:
- g(τ+2) = N·F(−1/τ) follows from η(τ+1) = e^{2πi/24}η(τ), η(−1/τ) = √(−iτ)η(τ) and Euler's
  η(w + ½) = e^{2πi/48}η(2w)³/(η(w)η(4w)) (with w = τ/4), in three lines;
- hence f|ST²S = (g|T²)|S = N·F|S² = N·F.
The S-defect of the would-be Q₈-orbifold character is then ¼(3N − T_ψ)(F − F|S), and F ≠ F|S because the
leading exponents n/48 and n/192 differ.

**S2. Reduction of the Main Question to k ≤ 24** (`notes/blueprint/README.md` §3, "Observation", which
the notes do not use). Holomorphic N=1 SVOAs are closed under ⊗, and for c ∈ 12ℤ the index is
multiplicative up to sign. So an index-1 theory W₂₄ at k = 24 gives W₂₄^{⊗q} ⊗ W_r (index 24/gcd(r,24))
for k = 24q + r. The Main Question is therefore a finite problem: twelve explicit (code, group) pairs
for k ∈ {3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 12, 15, 16, 18, 20, 21, 24} (codes of length ≤ 288) plus the Theorem 5.9 codes
for the other k ≤ 24. The chains remain the way to certify the index at length ≥ 96, but only finitely
many steps are needed. Worth one remark in the paper; it also tells a reader that nothing beyond
length 288 has to be trusted.

**S3. The J-chain is logically redundant.** Every code of the Q₈-chain (k ≡ 0 mod 4) contains J with
J² = −1 and all coordinate cycles of length 2, which is exactly what GJF Thm 5.6 needs for gcd(k,24) = 4.
So one family (Q₈) covers gcd ∈ {4, 8}. The J-chain from Golay can stay as the first, simplest example
of equivariance.

**S4. Seeds: only the length-72 seed needs a computer search.** Two experiments of this review
(scripts by helper agents, every reported number re-checked with fresh code in `review_seed_verify.py`):
- *gcd(k,24) ∈ {3, 6}: GJF's own k = 3 example* (GJF Ex 5.7, `codes/gjf_ex57_len36.txt`) with
  ℤ/6 = ⟨−h⟩, h = the shift by 12 [V: `review_gjf36_z6.py`, `review_seed_verify.py`].
  - Index = +24, T_h = 0 (known from GJF). Since h moves every block, every block stabiliser in ⟨−h⟩
    is {±1}: any placement and split can be transported along h-orbits, and neither the τ_J/τ_Q
    bookkeeping nor the transposed-split rule is needed for this family.
  - Good systems are plentiful: 803 of the 13,365 h-stable 3-block systems have R_σ = ±24 and
    R_σ̄(D) = 0 (7.2%; the ℤ/12 seed had 2 of 45), and about 1.5% of admissible 6-block systems
    (30 in a uniform sample of 2,000).
  - σ_A = {(0,25|2,5), (12,1|14,17), (24,13|26,29)} has R = +24 and twined value 0: chain k = 6, 12, 18, …
    with Index +24 (no sign flip). σ_B = {(2,15|28,21), (14,27|4,33), (26,3|16,9), (5,6|31,10),
    (17,18|7,22), (29,30|19,34)} has R = +24, twined 0: chain k = 9, 15, 21, …. Built and checked to
    n = 144 and n = 180; at n = 72, Index = +24 sign-exact and T_h = 0 by enumeration. A control system
    with R_σ̄(D) = −48 gives T_h = −48 at n = 72, a sign-exact test of Lemma 4.3.
  - m = 3 uses h, m = 6 uses −h ((−h)³ = −1, (−h)² = h²).
- *gcd(k,24) = 12 from the Q₈ × ℤ/3-chain* (k ≡ 0 mod 12): g = Jh has order 12, g⁶ = J² = −1, g⁴ = h of
  shape 3^{n/3} with T_h = 0, and Index = +24, so GJF Thm 5.6 applies as it stands. Together with the
  previous item, the searched ℤ/12 seed E₃₆ is not needed.
- *gcd(k,24) ∈ {4, 8}: the Golay code with a Q₈* [V: `review_golay_q8.py`, `review_seed_verify.py`].
  - Aut(Golay) = 2.M₁₂ contains 1320 subgroups Q₈ = ⟨J, K⟩ with J² = K² = −1 (one conjugacy class); each
    acts on the coordinates through a free Klein four-group, with three orbits of size 4. The J of the
    J-chain (`main-question.tex` §4.2) is one of them, with K = ((2,3,0,1,6,7,4,5,11,10,9,8),
    signs (−,+,+,−,−,−,+,+,+,−,+,−)).
  - **The obvious system fails:** with σ = the three Q₈-orbits, R_σ(Golay) = +72 for every Q₈. Proof
    (the agent's, checked): for a partition of the 12 coordinates into three 4-sets, R_σ = 24 − 48 + 8H,
    where H counts hexads that contain one block and meet the other two once. A hexad through an orbit
    that met another orbit in two points x, y would be stable under the element t ∈ Q₈ swapping x and y
    (as a set), so t·c = ±c for its word c; but t² = −1 then gives c = −c. So all 4 hexads through each
    orbit cross, H = 12 and R = 72.
  - **A mixed system works:** σ = {(0,1|2,3), (4,5|11,10), (6,7|8,9)}: one Q₈-orbit (type τ_Q, split into
    J-pairs) and two blocks each made of a J-pair of each remaining orbit (type τ_J, swapped by K). It
    is Q₈-stable with R_σ(Golay) = −24. The chain goes k = 1 → 4 → 8 → 12 …; at n = 48 the code is
    self-dual and Q₈-invariant with Index = −24 sign-exact (2²⁴ patterns, fresh code) and the next system
    has R = −24; n = 96 passes the structural checks. One diagonal sign change gives +24.
  - This replaces both the random length-24 Q₈ seed and the J-chain (J ∈ Q₈, S3).
  - Side result: the centraliser of each such Q₈ in Aut(Golay) is S₃ × ⟨−1⟩ and contains a fixed-point-free
    h of order 3 with T_h = 0, so Golay carries Q₈ × ℤ/3. It is useless for k ≡ 0 mod 24, since chains from
    Golay keep k ≡ 1 (mod 3) (and h preserves no mixed system).
- **Net effect:** seeds become the Golay code (gcd 1, 2, 4, 8), GJF's published k = 3 code (gcd 3, 6)
  and E₇₂ (gcd 12, 24). Only E₇₂ comes from a search of ours; the seeds `z12seed_len36_*` and
  `q8chain_len24` are no longer needed. The price for a Lean version: the certificates of GJF's code
  (2¹⁸ and ≈ 2¹²·3⁶ leaves) are larger than seed 6's; the formal proof as it stands need not change.

**S5. The Q₈ anomaly proof** needs only: cyclic restrictions (GJF Ex 4.4), |K| = 2 (counting), the
character argument, additivity (§3.3). No gauging lemma, no eta invariants, no μ.

**S6. One orbifold-index theorem for all groups used.** Let C have Index ±24 and G ⊂ Aut(C) with
(i) every abelian subgroup cyclic; (ii) if |G| is even, −1 ∈ G is its unique involution, and the
2-parts of elements have order ≤ 4; (iii) the odd-order elements form a central subgroup of order 1 or 3,
generated by h without fixed coordinate and with T_h = 0. If G is non-anomalous, Index(V//G) = ±24/|G|.
This covers GJF's cyclic groups, Q₈ and Q₈ × ℤ/3 at once (it is Theorem O with its Lemma 2.1 turned
into hypotheses), and makes clear that GJF Thm 5.6 is only cited for the anomaly.

**S7. Existence half, gadget form** (§2.2): optional.

## 6. Exposition and organisation

### 6.1 Two papers, not three

- **Paper I (combinatorics; complete, elementary, formally verified): the proof of Conjecture 1.4.**
  Merge route C of `divisibility.tex` with `index24.tex`. Both halves then have a short, self-contained
  proof, and the Lean theorem `GJF.conjecture_1_4` states exactly the paper's main theorem. The
  "four proofs" format of `divisibility.tex` is right for the notes but not for a journal paper.
- **Paper II (mathematical physics): the Main Question.** It depends on the fermionic orbifold theory
  of GJF §4, whose super-VOA proof is only sketched. Keeping it separate keeps Paper I's claims
  unconditional, and lets Paper II state its inputs once, up front.
- One combined paper is possible (Part I, Part II), but readers from coding theory would have to
  wade through anomalies, and referees for the physics part would have to referee combinatorics.

### 6.2 Paper I, outline (≈ 10–12 pages)

1. **Introduction.** GJF's index; Conjecture 1.4; what GJF proved (24 | n via Borcherds; k ≤ 5 by search;
   their remark that no elementary proof was known; Question 0.1). Theorem 1 (24 | Index, n ≥ 1) and
   Theorem 2 (index +24 at every length 12k, explicit). One paragraph of motivation (SVOA, TMF), one on
   the formalization.
2. **Notation.** φ, f, Fourier transform on F₃ⁿ, Poisson summation, weights of self-orthogonal codes.
3. **The signed 2-design.** Theorem (Σ s(ε)εᵢεⱼ = Index·δᵢⱼ) by route C; Proposition (design ⇒ 8 | Index
   and 3 | Index). Remarks: Golay ↔ Hadamard matrix of order 12; four marked coordinates (why strength 2
   is the limit); the modular proof and the lattice statement; the neighbour identity (★).
4. **Partial quadrupling.** Tetracode identities (Lemmas A, B); block systems and R_σ; Q_σ and its
   self-duality; main lemma; the gadget remark (S7).
5. **Index 24 in every length.** Base (Golay, σ₁, σ₂); engine; proof of Theorem 2; remarks: d_min ≥ 6,
   R_σ ≡ Index mod 48 (§2.3), first new lengths (72, 84), an explicit table or data file.
6. **Formal verification.** The Lean statement verbatim, axioms, what `decide` checks.
7. Optional short section "Further congruences": Theorem 5.4 (doubling preserves Index mod 48) and
   Proposition 5.5 (#even ≡ #odd ≡ 0 mod 8). Both are short and use the same neighbour technique; drop
   if space is tight.

What stays out: the Main Question, all exploratory material of notes §5.1(a)–(h) (gluing, rings,
transfer matrices, gadgets, seeds), the TMF discussion beyond one paragraph.

### 6.3 Paper II, outline (≈ 20–25 pages)

1. **Introduction.** Main Question; TMF bound; GJF Thm 5.6 and k ≤ 5. Main Theorem with the table
   below; the three ideas (build the symmetry into the construction; carry twined counts along as R of a
   collapsed code; for 8 | k replace ℤ/8, ℤ/24 by Q₈, Q₈ × ℤ/3); Theorem B (the cyclic obstruction) and
   Theorem C (the Q₈ anomaly); the list of inputs (§3.4); the reduction to k ≤ 24 (S2).
2. **Code SVOAs, orbifolds, anomalies.** V_{Λ(C)}, GJF's lift, Ramond ground states = maximal words;
   the anomaly groups and the inputs used.
3. **The orbifold index** (S6): Theorem O with the vacuum-energy lemma.
4. **Codes with symmetry.** Partial quadrupling recalled from Paper I; equivariance with the local types
   τ₀, τ_J, τ_Q and the transposed-split rule; twined counts via collapsed fixed codes; the chain theorem
   (index and T_h constant, lengths in an arithmetic progression).
5. **The chains.** One subsection per family, each with its seed certificate.
6. **Anomalies.** Cyclic (cite GJF; remark on G1–G3); Q₈ (Theorem C, proof of §3.3); Q₈ × ℤ/3.
7. **Proof of the Main Theorem** (half a page: the table).
8. **The cyclic obstruction** (Theorem B, Hermitian four-code identity). Not needed for the Main
   Theorem, but it explains why a non-cyclic group is forced for 8 | k.
9. **Remarks.** Morita form of Q₈-invariant codes and why the Q₈ × ℤ/3 seed has length 72 (the observed
   72-law at length 36, stated as an observation); α = 3k·a via free fermions and eta invariants on S³/Q₈;

### 6.4 Style points for both papers

- State the sign convention once (index up to sign; normalise to +24 by one diagonal sign change, which
  preserves every symmetry property used) and then stop tracking signs.
- Give every seed as data in the paper or an ancillary file, with the exact sum that certifies it.
- Keep GJF's numbering with the footnote that it refers to arXiv v1 (the CJM numbering was not checked).
- In Paper II, mark every statement that depends on the orbifold framework, e.g. by stating the
  framework once as "Hypothesis ORB" and writing "(under ORB)" in the theorems that use it.
- Figures would help in two places: the coordinate layout of Q_σ for one block (4 → 8 → 16, with the
  refined blocks), and the lattice of subgroups of Q₈ × ℤ/3 with the twisted-sector argument.
