## 1. Report on Paper I (`conj14.tex`)

### Summary

The paper proves both halves of Gaiotto–Johnson-Freyd's Conjecture 1.4 on self-dual ternary codes:
- **Theorem A**: 24 | Index(C) for every self-dual code of positive length. The proof shows that the maximal codewords form a signed 2-design, using one finite Poisson summation, and then gets the factors 8 and 3 by counting.
- **Theorem B**: there is an explicit code of index 24 in every length 12k. The tool is *partial quadrupling* Q_σ, which glues tetracodes onto a block system twice. It turns the block-weighted index R_σ into the index (main lemma, Lemma 4.6) and reproduces itself after one refinement.

It then makes the construction equivariant (§6) and builds, from three seeds (Golay, GJF's E₃₆, a new E₇₂), codes with the automorphisms that the orbifold constructions of the Main Question need:
- **Theorem C** and its Corollary (GJF Thm 5.6 applies when 8 ∤ k);
- **Theorem D**: g⁴ = −1 forces 48 | Index, so GJF's cyclic construction fails when 8 | k.

Complements: R_σ ≡ Index (mod 48), and 8 divides both #even and #odd. Theorems A and B are formalised in Lean.

### Assessment

- **Correctness.** All results are correct for codes of positive length, and every proof is complete. (Three statements need "positive length" added; see Main comment 4.) I re-derived:
  - Thm 3.1 with Lemma 3.2 (the factor −1/3), and Prop 3.3 (the triple identity and the test vectors ε⁰ and ε⁰ + 3ε⁰₁e₁);
  - Lemmas 4.1 and 4.2, and the self-duality of Q_σ(E) (the form becomes ⟨e,e′⟩ + ⟨t₁,t₁′⟩ − ⟨t₂,t₂′⟩);
  - the main lemma, the engine and the proof of Thm B;
  - Lemma 5.4 (R = 8H − 24);
  - §6: Lemma 6.2 (both models), Construction 6.3, Lemmas 6.4–6.5, Prop 6.6;
  - Props 7.1–7.4, Thm C and its Corollary (element by element against GJF's statement of Thm 5.6);
  - Thm D (factorisation of x⁴ + 1, isotropy of V±, the 32 isotropic vectors = 4 isotropic F₉-lines, the four-code identity, g exchanging C₃ and C₄);
  - Lemmas 9.1–9.2, Props 9.4–9.5;
  - the M₂(F₃) coordinate formula of App. A.2.

  All printed data and every computed value were checked independently (see the last subsection). No error was found.
- **Novelty and significance.** Theorem A closes the case n ≡ 12 (mod 24), which GJF left open. The proof uses no modular forms, which answers GJF's remark that they did not know an elementary proof. Theorem B answers GJF's Question 0.1 (no intensive computation: the only check is a 729-word sum). Theorems C and D are the code-theoretic half of the Main Question and are new.
  - A web search (2026-10-10) found no prior solution of Conjecture 1.4.
  - It also found no other construction of index-24 codes in every length.
- **Presentation.** Dense but well organised; the proofs are short and the main ideas are visible. Several remarks overreach, a few statements miss the positive-length hypothesis, the F₉-structure in §8 is introduced in an order that obscures it, and some appendix facts are asserted without the one-line reason.
- **Recommendation: accept after minor revision.**

### Main comments

1. **The abstract overclaims what Theorem D excludes** (l.53–54: "Cyclic groups cannot work when 8 | k").
   - Theorem D only excludes an automorphism with g⁴ = −1. So it rules out GJF's construction: an index-24 code with a cyclic group of order gcd(k,24), as the body correctly says (l.153–159).
   - It does not exclude other cyclic routes. For example, take k = 16 and a code of index 48 with an automorphism of order 16 and g⁸ = −1:
     - Theorem D permits it, since 48 | 48;
     - its cyclic anomaly 2kν₁₆ = 32ν₁₆ vanishes in ℤ/32;
     - Paper II's Thm 1.4 would then give index 48/16 = 3.
   - Fix: "the cyclic construction of [GJF, Thm 5.6] cannot work when 8 | k".
2. **Scope statements in the abstract and introduction.**
   - (a) "The construction commutes with code automorphisms that permute its blocks" (l.48–49, l.128) is broader than Lemma 6.4. That lemma needs a group containing −1, mapping pairs to pairs, with every block of type τ₀, τ_J or τ_Q.
     - The remark after Lemma 6.4 says the rule fails otherwise; I confirmed that no tetracode is invariant under (p q)(p* q*) with equal signs or under diag(1,−1,1,−1).
   - (b) For 8 | k the abstract (l.49–53) reads as an existence claim for orbifolds. Two things are needed and are not in this paper:
     - the non-anomaly of the Q₈ and Q₈ × ℤ/3 actions;
     - the index 3, resp. 1, of the orbifolds (l.164–165). GJF's Thm 5.6 covers only cyclic groups.

     Both are proved in Paper II, under Hypothesis ORB. Cite [II] at both places, and say that the vertex-algebra statements are conditional.
3. **Prop 7.4 does not state what Theorem C(c) uses.** Its conclusion (l.806–808) gives only Index(𝒬𝒵_k) = 24 and "J, K, h with the relations above". Theorem C(c) also needs T_h(𝒬𝒵_k) = 0, and the proof of Theorem C (l.818) cites Prop 7.4 alone.
   - The missing fact follows from Prop 6.6(4) and R^h_{σ_QZ}(E₇₂) = 0, which is stated in Prop 7.4.
   - Fix: add "T_h(𝒬𝒵_k) = 0" to the conclusion, as Prop 7.3 does for 𝒵_k.
4. **Missing positive-length hypotheses.** The introduction stresses that the length-0 code has index 1 (l.100–101), but three statements omit the hypothesis and are false at length 0:
   - Prop 3.3 (l.288–289): "Theorem 3.1 implies 8 | Index and 3 | Index";
   - Prop 9.5 (l.922–924): "in every self-dual ternary code" — the length-0 code has exactly one (even) maximal word;
   - Theorem D (l.155–157): at k = 0, Mon(0) is trivial, so −1 = 1 and g = id satisfies g⁴ = −1, while Index = 1.

   Fix: add "of positive length", resp. "k ≥ 1".
5. **Remark 3.4(3) asserts more than it shows.** It says the modular-form argument "uses only the lattice, and gives 24 | Σ s(v) over the shadow vectors of norm k of every odd unimodular lattice of rank 12k whose shadow has minimum ≥ k".
   - s(v) is not defined for a general lattice.
   - The step from the 2-design to 24 | Σ is not "the same" as in Prop 3.3, which uses the frame coordinates and congruences mod 3 in ℤⁿ.

   The claim does appear to be true; one reader supplied a lattice-intrinsic argument, which I checked:
   - Put s(v) = (−1)^{⟨v − χ/2, χ⟩}. The 2-design reads Σ s(v) v vᵀ = (I/12)·1.
   - For μ in the even sublattice L₀, v·μ ∈ ℤ; pairing ±v gives I μ² ∈ 24ℤ. A μ ∈ L₀ with 3 ∤ μ² exists (otherwise L₀ ⊆ 3L₀*, impossible since det L₀ = 4), so 3 | I.
   - For odd μ, v·μ ∈ ½ + ℤ, which gives I(μ² − 3) ∈ 48ℤ. An odd μ with μ² ≡ 1 (mod 4) exists for n ≥ 3, so 8 | I.

   Fix: define s(v) and add this sketch, or restrict the remark to the 2-design statement.
6. **E₇₂: provenance and justification** (§7.3, App. A.2).
   - (a) Say how E₇₂ was found (a random search over Lagrangians U for the Morita form?). This is the one seed that does not come from GJF or the Golay code. GJF's Question 0.1 asks for constructions without intensive computation, so the reader should know how the seed was obtained and that its value R_{σ_QZ} = 24 needs a 2³⁶-term computation. Give that number in the introduction rather than only in §10. Also report that the value has now been confirmed by four independent programs.
   - (b) h ∈ Aut(E₇₂) is asserted without justification. It amounts to U being invariant under the block permutation (2b, 2b+1) ↦ (2h(b), 2h(b)+1), with h acting inside each block by the identity. That holds for the printed U, but state it.
   - (c) "Self-dual because U is Lagrangian" needs the block identity ⟨X, X′⟩ = −(ω(x₁, x₂′) + ω(x₁′, x₂)), where x₁, x₂ are the rows of X. It follows from det = reduced norm (proof of Lemma 6.2); write the line.
7. **Exposition of the F₉-structure in the proof of Theorem D** (l.837–861).
   - The text first makes V₊ an F₉-space through g (a scalar λ), then says J = g² acts as λ² = √−1 on all of Fⁿ, and then that g acts on L± by λ and −λ. These statements are consistent only once F₉ is defined through J.
   - Suggest setting F₉ = F₃[J], i := J, from the start, and recording:
     - J = 1 − g on V₊ and J = g + 1 on V₋;
     - so g = 1 − i on V₊ and g = −(1 − i) on V₋;
     - and λ := 1 − i satisfies λ² = i.
   - State the step "ternary-isotropic ⟺ Hermitian-isotropic", which "32 isotropic vectors = 4 lines" needs.
   - Give the one line for the swap: g(u + au′) = λ(u − au′).
   - Optionally, make the four-code identity a lemma. It holds for every J-invariant D of codimension 2.

## 2. Report on Paper II (`minimal-index.tex`)

### Summary

The paper answers GJF's Main Question for every k: there is a holomorphic N=1 vertex operator superalgebra of central charge 12k and index 24/gcd(k,24). It is V_{Λ(C)}//G for a code C from Paper I and a group G as in Table 1. The result is conditional on Hypothesis ORB (the fermionic orbifold theorem, sketched in GJF) and on the cited inputs (A1)–(A10). The new ingredients are three theorems.
- **Thm 1.2**: a quaternion group of code automorphisms is non-anomalous iff 8 | k.
  - The cyclic restrictions vanish iff 4 | k.
  - The one remaining class κ is detected by the non-S-invariance of a would-be orbifold character at k ≡ 4 (mod 8). That character is built from the (−1)-twisted sector, which as a projective Q₈-module is a Clifford spinor module independent of C.
  - The case 8 | k then follows by additivity over C ⊕ (tetracode sum).
- **Thm 1.3**: Q₈ × ℤ/3 is non-anomalous iff 24 | k, by Sylow detection.
- **Thm 1.4**: the orbifold index equals ±Index(C)/|G| for every group with cyclic abelian subgroups, −1 as its only involution, and a central odd part of order ≤ 3 generated by h with T_h = 0.

§3 reproves the anomaly half of GJF Thm 5.6, with two misprints corrected and one citation replaced by a proof. Remarks cover free fermions (anomaly 3k·a, with a of order 8), the reduction to k ≤ 24, and the TMF meaning of the k = 24 theory.
